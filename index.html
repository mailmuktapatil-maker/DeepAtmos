How DeepAtmos Solves the Generalization Gap:

Traditional Bayesian retrievals easily get biased when correlated noise or starspots tilt the spectral continuum, producing false-positive water or carbon claims. DeepAtmos uses multi-scale 1D dilated convolutions with receptive field kernels that look across local line shapes rather than global tilt, preserving high detection fidelity even under 3× nominal observational noise.

Spectral Saliency & Feature Attribution

Inspect the exact wavelength channels that triggered the neural network's molecular identification.

🧠 Neural Receptive Field Attribution

DeepAtmos is not a black box. The framework computes gradient activation weights across the 128 spectral bins, identifying precisely which quantum absorption features dictated the prediction:

Carbon Dioxide (CO₂): Peak attribution at 4.18 – 4.45 µm (Fundamental ν₃ asymmetric stretch).
Water Vapor (H₂O): Multi-band attribution at 1.35 – 1.48 µm, 1.80 – 2.05 µm, and 2.60 – 2.95 µm.
Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂): Highly localized attribution at 3.95 – 4.12 µm (Photochemical tracer).
Methane (CH₄): Receptive activation at 2.25 – 2.45 µm and 3.20 – 3.45 µm.

🔬 Solving the Black-Box Retrieval Problem

In astronomical peer review, scientists reject AI predictions if the model cannot explain why a detection was made. DeepAtmos solves this by overlaying the Saliency Heatmap directly onto the observational data points in the Spectral Studio.
